Search Intelligence Research Project

Finding Content Opportunities from Search Signals

A practical ML project for prioritizing content pages that may deserve human review using search-performance signals.

FlyRank ML Internship March 2026 working data Search Intelligence
ABSTRACT

What this project asks

This project asks whether search-performance signals can be used to prioritize content pages for human review. I used March 2026 search data and built a simple rule-based baseline followed by a Logistic Regression model. The model was evaluated first with a random split and then with a client-grouped split to make the validation more realistic. The Logistic Regression model reached an F1 score of 0.944 on the random split and 0.943 on the client-grouped split, while the Week-4 rule-based baseline had an F1 score of 1.0 on its evaluation setup. The result should be treated as decision-support evidence rather than proof that the recommendations will improve search performance.

01 — INTRODUCTION

Problem statement

Content teams can have a large number of pages to review. Looking at every page manually is difficult, so a useful first step is to identify pages that have measurable search signals and may deserve attention.

The goal of this project is not to automatically decide which pages should be changed. Instead, the goal is to create a ranked queue that helps a human reviewer decide where to start.

Research question: Can observable search-performance signals be used to build a useful prioritization system for content review?

The practical decision supported by the project is: which content pages should a reviewer investigate first?

02 — DATA

Data used

The analysis uses the FlyRank ML Internship warehouse dataset. The work focuses on the monthly content-performance data available for March 2026.

9.84M March 2026 rows before availability filtering
3.61M rows with GSC data available
March 2026 main working window

Main signals

  • Search impressions
  • Search clicks
  • Average search position
  • Google Search Console availability
  • Content and client identifiers used only for grouping and analysis

What was excluded

Client names, domains, private queries, credentials, and other identifying information were excluded from the public-facing analysis.

Future-window information and label-derived information were also excluded from model features because they could introduce leakage.

The final month of the warehouse was treated as a sealed outcome month rather than a development month. March 2026 was therefore used as the working month for the main analysis.

03 — METHODOLOGY

From signals to a review queue

Unit of analysis

The analysis treats a row as a content-performance observation for a reporting date and client/content combination.

Baseline

Before using a trained model, I created a simple rule-based prioritization baseline. It uses observable search signals to rank pages and assign reason codes.

Low CTR opportunity

Pages with meaningful impressions but a low click-through rate receive a CTR review reason.

Low position

Pages with meaningful impressions and weaker average position receive a content review reason.

High opportunity

Other pages with measurable visibility remain available for monitoring and review.

Model

I used Logistic Regression as the first trained model. The choice was intentional: it is relatively simple, fast, and easier to interpret than a more complex model.

The model was not selected because it was the most complicated option. The purpose was to test whether a simple learned model could provide useful performance compared with the existing baseline.

Validation

The Week-5 model was initially evaluated with a stratified random train/test split. In Week 6, the same modeling approach was evaluated using a client-grouped split to reduce the possibility that observations from the same client would appear across both training and evaluation data.

Leakage checks

Features were reviewed for information that would only become available after the decision point. Future-window information and label-derived features were excluded from the final feature set.

Interpretation

The model's F1 score changed only slightly when moving from the random split to the client-grouped split. This is useful evidence that the observed model performance was not dramatically dependent on the original random split.

However, the baseline score of 1.0 means the baseline comparison needs to be interpreted carefully. A perfect score in the baseline setup does not automatically mean that the rule will perform perfectly on unseen future data.

Important: These results measure performance on the evaluation setups used in this project. They do not prove that the model or rule will improve rankings, clicks, traffic, or business outcomes after a content change.

05 — LIMITATIONS

What this work cannot claim

This project is a decision-support experiment, not a production content optimization system.

  • It cannot prove that changing a page will improve search traffic.
  • It cannot prove a causal relationship between a recommendation and a future ranking change.
  • The score does not understand content quality or search intent directly.
  • The score does not know the business importance of a page.
  • Search performance can change because of factors outside the signals used in this project.
  • The analysis is based on the available warehouse data and its coverage, so missing or unavailable data can affect conclusions.

Honest framing

The safest interpretation is that the observed signals can help prioritize human investigation. They should not be interpreted as automatic instructions or guarantees of improvement.

06 — RANKED RECOMMENDATIONS

Content action playbook

The ranked queue is designed to answer one practical question: which pages should a reviewer look at first?

01 — Review CTR

Start with pages that have meaningful search impressions but relatively few clicks.

Reason: LOW_CTR_OPPORTUNITY

Review the title, search snippet, and alignment between the visible result and search intent.

02 — Review content

Review pages that have search visibility but relatively weak average position.

Reason: LOW_POSITION

A reviewer should inspect content quality, relevance, and search intent before deciding whether a change is appropriate.

03 — Monitor

Pages without a strong review signal can remain on a monitoring list.

Reason: HIGH_OPPORTUNITY

Monitoring avoids making unnecessary changes based only on limited signals.

Human review is required

Every recommended action should be reviewed by a person before a content change is made.

  • Check whether the page matches the search intent.
  • Check whether the content is factually correct.
  • Check whether the title and snippet are appropriate.
  • Consider the business importance of the page.
  • Consider seasonality or temporary changes.
  • Check for legal, factual, or brand risks.

What should not be automated

  • Automatically publishing content changes.
  • Deleting or redirecting pages.
  • Changing legal or regulated content.
  • Changing important business claims.
  • Declaring a page "bad" from the score alone.
  • Guaranteeing that an SEO change will improve performance.

Monitoring and review triggers

The system should be reviewed if the input data structure changes, search-signal distributions change substantially, model performance drops under the same validation design, or human reviewers frequently reject the highest-ranked recommendations.

08 — ARTIFACTS

Main project artifacts

The project produces a ranked action queue from the notebook rather than maintaining a manually edited data file. The queue contains the search signals, prioritization score, reason code, action, and rank used for review.

The ranked queue is intended to be regenerated from the notebook. This keeps the analysis reproducible instead of treating the CSV as a manually maintained source of truth.
